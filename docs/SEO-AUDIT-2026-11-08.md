# Full SEO audit — Élan Patrimoine

Audit date: 8 November 2026 (Africa/Casablanca)  
Live site: <https://elanpatrimoines.fr>  
Code audited: current working tree in `C:\Users\medes\Projects\Anis`  
Audit mode: read-only application review, live crawl, rendered-DOM checks, structured-data extraction, mobile lab trace, typecheck, and production build. No application source was changed by this audit.

## Executive verdict

**Do not actively request indexing until the P0 items below are closed.** The live site is technically crawlable, but it currently publishes inaccurate/demo legal identity, social proof, authorship, and experience claims in a financial/YMYL context. The production deployment is also materially behind the working tree: many corrections already exist locally but are uncommitted and unreleased.

The most urgent risk is not a missing meta tag. It is trust consistency:

- Production identifies the company as an **SAS**, gives **RCS Paris 901 234 567** and **€50,000 capital**, while the verified/current source identifies a **SARL**, SIREN **106 362 254**, created **5 June 2026**, with **€1,000 capital**.
- Production claims **8 years of experience**, **99% satisfaction**, **100% independent**, and **+200 engagements**, and publishes six named testimonials plus partner/client logos. The current source explicitly removes all of these until they can be evidenced.
- All 12 live financial articles use the demo bylines **Antoine Gérard** or **Sophie Laurent**. The current source explicitly identifies those names as legacy demo data that must not be presented as real people.
- Those articles contain only **84–189 body words** while claiming **5–8 minutes** of reading time. They have no contextual internal or external links in the article body.
- All 12 articles predate the company's official creation date. Content may legitimately predate a company, but only if authorship, provenance, original publication, and review history are truthful and disclosed.

Google's people-first guidance asks whether content has clear sourcing, demonstrable expertise, author/site background, and easily verifiable facts. These live inconsistencies work directly against those trust signals: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content>.

## P0 — close before requesting indexing

### P0-1. Correct all identity and regulated-activity claims

Live pages and global JSON-LD publish stale/demo company data. The homepage/footer and legal page include “Élan Patrimoine SAS” and “RCS Paris 901 234 567”; the legal page also claims €50,000 capital. The official company record for SIREN 106 362 254 reports a SARL created 5 June 2026 at 231 rue Saint-Honoré: <https://annuaire-entreprises.data.gouv.fr/entreprise/106362254>.

Actions:

1. Deploy the working-tree identity corrections.
2. Purge CDN/Next caches.
3. Search rendered HTML, RSC payloads, metadata, schema, email templates, CMS settings, and tracking payloads for the old legal name, RCS, capital, and social URLs.
4. Verify ORIAS number, exact registered categories, professional indemnity details, mediator(s), supervisory wording, and the meaning of “independent” against current official records and compliance counsel before publication.
5. Keep immutable legal fields out of editable CMS settings, as the current `lib/siteInfo.ts` design already does.

### P0-2. Remove or substantiate every proof/experience claim

Production exposes:

- 8 years of experience
- 100% independent
- 99% satisfaction
- +200 engagements
- six named testimonials
- multiple partner/client logos and Google-review visual cues

These conflict with a company founded in June 2026 unless they refer to a named professional's prior experience and are precisely attributed. Testimonials, satisfaction claims, engagement counts, and logos need source records, methodology/date, consent, and usage rights.

Actions:

- Deploy the current empty stats/testimonials and disabled-logo defaults immediately.
- If a founder has eight years' personal experience, state it as the person's experience on a verifiable profile page, with dates and credentials—not as the company's age.
- Define “independent” precisely (capital ownership, product architecture, and/or regulatory remuneration model) and disclose compensation.
- Do not publish a testimonial, rating, logo, client count, or satisfaction percentage without an evidence register and expiry/review date.

### P0-3. Repair financial-content provenance

All 12 articles currently expose one of two demo people as a `Person` author. This is especially damaging for financial/YMYL content. The source itself confirms these are legacy demo names at `lib/articles.ts:51-59,145-149` and blocks them in `sanity/schemaTypes/articleType.ts:117-127`.

Actions:

- Until rewritten and reviewed, either remove/noindex the demo articles or clearly mark them as drafts outside production.
- Use a real author or reviewer with name, role, relevant credentials, profile URL, and editorial responsibility.
- Where the organization is the true author, say so honestly, but add a named expert reviewer when advice touches tax, investment, insurance, inheritance, or regulation.
- Add `datePublished`, genuine `dateModified`, “reviewed by”, source list, limitations, risk disclosures, and a correction policy.
- Resolve pre-incorporation dates through truthful provenance: original publisher/author, original date, migration/review date. Do not backdate company publications.

### P0-4. Replace thin/demo articles

The problem is not a magic word count. It is that the live articles make consequential financial claims without enough explanation, examples, primary sources, caveats, or decision support. Google explicitly says it has no preferred word count; value and completeness matter.

Every article should answer the actual decision intent, use current primary sources (legislation, BOFiP, AMF, ACPR, Service-Public, Banque de France as applicable), show assumptions/examples, explain risks and exceptions, and link to the relevant service and related explanatory content.

The declared reading time must be calculated from rendered words. At roughly 200–250 words/minute, 84–189 words cannot honestly be labeled 5–8 minutes.

### P0-5. Fix the canonical host at infrastructure level

Observed:

- `http://elanpatrimoines.fr` → 302 to HTTPS apex.
- `https://elanpatrimoines.fr` → 200.
- `http://www.elanpatrimoines.fr` → 404.
- `https://www.elanpatrimoines.fr` → 503 with a default Traefik certificate.

Because HSTS includes subdomains, the broken HTTPS `www` host is especially important. `next.config.mjs:168-190` contains the correct application redirect intent, but it cannot run until the hosting layer accepts the hostname and has a valid certificate.

Actions:

1. Bind `www.elanpatrimoines.fr` to the production service.
2. Issue a valid certificate for `www`.
3. Return one-hop permanent 308/301 redirects from both HTTP and HTTPS `www` to the same apex path/query.
4. Prefer a permanent redirect for HTTP apex as well.

### P0-6. Eliminate soft 404s and pagination crawl traps

Live invalid article URLs return HTTP 200, inherit the homepage canonical, and can emit conflicting robots directives. Current code still permits this because `generateMetadata()` returns `{}` when the article is missing at `app/(site)/actualites/[slug]/page.tsx:133-140`; `notFound()` is called only later at lines 166–168, after streaming can begin.

Fix: call `notFound()` inside `generateMetadata()` when the article is absent, and retain the page-level guard. Verify a true HTTP 404, one `noindex` directive, no homepage canonical, and the custom 404 UI. Google recommends returning 404/410 for missing pages: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/troubleshoot-crawling-errors>.

Live archive behavior is also wrong:

- `?page=1`: 200 duplicate instead of a redirect to `/actualites`.
- `?page=2`: 200 but canonical points to `/actualites`, collapsing the only archive page for four older articles.
- `?page=3`, `?page=999`, `?page=0`, `?page=-1`, `?page=abc`, and duplicate `page` parameters: 200 duplicates.

The working-tree parser, self-canonical logic, and middleware in `app/(site)/actualites/page.tsx:14-82` and `middleware.ts:77-105` are directionally correct. Google recommends a distinct URL and self-canonical for each real pagination page: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/pagination-and-incremental-page-loading>.

### P0-7. Deploy the editorial policy or remove its links

`/politique-editoriale` is linked from the footer and every current article implementation, but production returns a custom 404/noindex and omits it from the live sitemap. Deploy the new route, footer links, article links, metadata, and sitemap entry in the same release.

### P0-8. Control the indexability switch deliberately

`lib/seo.ts:48-53,79-81` makes every page `noindex,nofollow` unless `SITE_INDEXABLE` is exactly `true`. This is a reasonable pre-launch gate but a severe deployment foot-gun.

Required release sequence:

1. Deploy corrected content with `SITE_INDEXABLE=false`.
2. Crawl production and obtain compliance/content sign-off.
3. Confirm all canonical pages, errors, host redirects, schema, and sitemap.
4. Set `SITE_INDEXABLE=true` and redeploy.
5. Assert `index,follow` on a fixed URL set in CI/post-deploy monitoring.

- Robots allows the public site and references the absolute sitemap.

Findings:

- The Google verification file returns 200, but that does not prove the correct Search Console property is active or that the sitemap is accepted.
- Production robots disallows `/studio` but not `/api/`; the current `app/robots.ts:4-14` adds `/api/`.
- `/studio` returns 200/noindex and is disallowed. Keep it authenticated/secured; robots is not access control.
- API routes receive `X-Robots-Tag: noindex,nofollow` in current `next.config.mjs:199-202`.

### Sitemap

Live sitemap composition: 9 core/static pages, 4 expertise pages, and 12 articles = 25 URLs.

Passes:

- Absolute canonical URLs only.
- No 3xx/4xx/5xx URLs in the live sitemap at audit time.
- Article URLs are discoverable even though archive pagination is flawed.

Findings:

- Live static entries all use the same stale/fabricated `lastmod` (`2026-06-13`).
- Live article `lastmod` equals publication date rather than real modification date.
- Current `app/sitemap.ts:36-44` removes fake static dates and uses Sanity `_updatedAt`, which is better.
- `changeFrequency` and `priority` are harmless, but Google largely ignores them.
- If Sanity fails, `lib/articles.ts:189-208` returns `[]`, and `app/sitemap.ts:36-46` can emit/cache a sitemap with every article removed. Preserve last-known-good URLs or fail/alert when expected CMS content disappears.
- The local production build passed despite Sanity fetch failures and generated no article static params. This confirms the integrity risk is real.

### Canonicals and URL normalization

Passes:

- All 25 sitemap pages expose absolute apex canonicals.
- UTM variants canonicalize to the clean URL.
- Trailing-slash variants redirect with 308.
- Unknown uppercase routes return 404.

Findings:

- Pagination canonicals are wrong in production (P0 above).
- Unknown article routes are soft 404s with the homepage canonical.
- Standard 404 pages inherit the root canonical from `app/layout.tsx:41`; error pages should omit canonical metadata.
- The root canonical may serialize with or without a trailing slash depending on the layer. Keep all signals consistent, though search engines normally normalize this pair.

### Metadata and headings

Passes:

- No missing or duplicate titles/descriptions were found among sitemap URLs.
- Every sitemap page has exactly one H1.
- Core-page descriptions are generally descriptive and 117–160 characters.
- Heading hierarchies on sampled public pages were coherent.

Findings:

- Eleven of 12 live article titles are longer than 60 characters including the brand (59–77 range). Length is not a ranking rule, but truncation/rewriting risk is high. Current `seoTitle` and absolute-title support at `app/(site)/actualites/[slug]/page.tsx:142-158` plus CMS warning at `sanity/schemaTypes/articleType.ts:31-38` should be deployed.
- Keep title, H1, `og:title`, and major anchor text closely aligned; Google uses all of them when generating title links: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link>.
- Nine live core/static pages have no `og:image` or `twitter:image`. Current `lib/seo.ts:59-98` correctly supplies a fallback.

- Breadcrumb markup matches visible breadcrumbs.
- Current source improves organization identity, logo, IDs, author type, dates, and publisher linkage.

Findings:

### Internal linking

Passes:

- All 32 unique internal targets discovered in the live crawl returned direct 200 responses, except the separately tested editorial-policy link.
- Main navigation, breadcrumbs, service cross-links, article cards, and related-article blocks use crawlable anchors.

Findings:

- Live article bodies contain **zero contextual internal links** and **zero external citations**.
- The CMS link field uses Sanity `url` and only accepts `http`, `https`, `mailto`, and `tel` at `sanity/schemaTypes/blockContentType.ts:31-44`, preventing root-relative internal links.
- `components/PortableBody.tsx:46-56` treats every absolute HTTP(S) URL—including the site's own domain—as external and opens it in a new tab.
- Add an internal-page reference annotation or safely allow `/...`, normalize same-origin absolute links to internal Next links, and preserve normal same-tab behavior.
- `components/ArticleCard.tsx:30-35,63-69,107-113` uses the article title as image alt text even though CMS `imageAlt` exists. Because image and title are in the same link, either use the real `imageAlt` when the image adds information or `alt=""` when it is redundant/decorative.

### Publication controls

- Sanity queries at `sanity/lib/queries.ts:24-27` do not filter future `date` values. If scheduled publishing is intended, apply a consistent `date <= now()` rule to list, detail, featured, sitemap, static params, and middleware article counts.
- Add validation that `datePublished <= dateModified <= now()` unless a documented scheduled-release workflow exists.
- Compute reading time from rendered Portable Text at publish/build time rather than entering it manually.
- Add content states (`draft`, `reviewed`, `published`, `archived`) and block production publication without author/reviewer/source/alt/meta fields.

This single throttled run is directional and included a very slow cold/revalidation TTFB. Obtain CrUX/Search Console 75th-percentile field values before declaring a Core Web Vitals pass/fail. Google's “good” thresholds are LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms, and CLS ≤0.1 at the 75th percentile: <https://web.dev/articles/defining-core-web-vitals-thresholds>.

- Decorative hero/section videos compete for bandwidth.
- Numerous client components and animations increase hydration/main-thread work.
- Three font families and many weights produce a sizable font set.
- Homepage HTML is roughly 215 KB uncompressed and live decoded script volume was roughly 716 KB in the trace.

- `components/Preloader.tsx` now renders nothing.
- `components/useDeferredVideo.ts` defers decorative media, respects reduced motion/data saver/slow connections, and can require interaction.
- Hero video remains a static optimized poster on mobile and delays multi-megabyte video on desktop.
- Slideshow delays loading the next slide beyond the LCP window.
- Next Image serves AVIF/WebP.

### Remaining performance actions

1. Deploy current video/preloader changes and rerun Lighthouse mobile three times per template plus WebPageTest.
2. Capture Search Console/CrUX field data for home, service, article, and contact groups.
3. Reduce client boundaries/animation code and lazy-load below-fold widgets; the chatbot is already dynamically loaded.
4. Audit font usage and remove unused weights/family subsets; preload only the true above-fold fonts.
5. Set performance budgets in CI: route JS, image bytes, LCP resource, request count, and long-task total.
6. Configure `images.qualities` for the used `quality={65}` before Next 16; the build currently warns this will be required.
7. Monitor cache HIT/MISS/STALE and Sanity latency so revalidation never produces multi-second TTFB.
